Applying Derivatives to Malaysian Palm Oil Extraction

Interactive App: Optimizing Harvest Windows using $f'(x)$ Rate of Change

🌱 Bioscience Case Study

In Malaysia's palm oil sector, maximizing the Oil Extraction Rate (OER) is the core objective of mills. Oil synthesis in the fruit mesocarp (pulp) escalates drastically after 120 days post-anthesis (flowering).

By plotting oil accumulation as a logistic growth curve, we use the first derivative $f'(x)$ to calculate the precise rate of oil synthesis ($\%/\text{day}$). This tells food tech managers exactly when metabolic synthesis slows down, marking the optimum harvest window before over-ripening and destructive Free Fatty Acid (FFA) buildup begins.

Data parameters adapted from MPOB technical models & academic literature (e.g., Southworth et al.; Siregar et al.).

🧮 Mathematical Modeling

Total Oil Content Function $f(x)$:
f(x) = L / (1 + e^(-k * (x - x0)))

Where Max Oil L = 52%, Growth rate k = 0.12, Mid-point x0 = 130 days.

The Derivative $f'(x)$ (Chain Rule):
f'(x) = k * f(x) * (1 - f(x)/L)

Measures the instantaneous rate of oil production per single day.
