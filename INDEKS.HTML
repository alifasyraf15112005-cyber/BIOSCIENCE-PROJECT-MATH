🌾 The Bioscience Context

In Malaysian agro-product industries, predicting the exact optimal harvest time for oil palm (*Elaeis guineensis*) biomass is vital for processing efficiency.

While cumulative growth follows an **S-shaped Sigmoid curve**, a manager needs the **derivative $f'(x)$** to know exactly when the tree hits its maximum *speed* of production. This represents peak biological efficiency before growth hits a plateau.

🧮 Mathematical Model

A logistic growth model scaled to real-world Malaysian commercial standards (0 to 12 years post-planting).

Cumulative Biomass $f(x)$:
$$f(x) = \frac{35}{1 + e^{-0.8(x - 5.5)}}$$
Yields total dry biomass weight in tonnes ($t$) per hectare.
Instantaneous Growth Rate $f'(x)$:
$$f'(x) = \frac{28e^{-0.8(x - 5.5)}}{(1 + e^{-0.8(x - 5.5)})^2}$$
Calculates the precise rate of biomass accumulation in **tonnes/hectare/year** at year $x$.
📊 Reference & Data Baseline: Modeled based on baseline physiological parameters from the **Malaysian Palm Oil Board (MPOB)** webpage *“Oil Palm Biomass Statistics”* and growth dynamics detailed in *“Journal of Oil Palm Research (JOPR)”*.
